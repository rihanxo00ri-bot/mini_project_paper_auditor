**Short answer:**  
The authors discovered a *compact, four‑port MIMO antenna* built from **concentric square‑ring microstrip patches** that are *loaded with Complementary Split‑Ring Resonators (CSRR)* on the ground plane.  By etching the CSRRs at the points of strongest coupling their ground‑current polarity flips (≈ 180°) and thereby **reduces mutual coupling by ≈ 6.5 dB**, giving an overall **isolation of ≥ –22 dB** between the identically polarized elements.  The design simultaneously provides **polarization and angle (diversity)** through orthogonal higher‑order modes, achieves a **wide 2:1 VSWR bandwidth (~75 MHz, up to 80 MHz after slot loading)**, and yields **low envelope correlation (< 0.4)** in the operating band.  An experimental prototype confirmed the simulations: measured S‑parameters show the predicted –6.5 dB coupling reduction, peak gains of 4 dBi (inner ring) / 3.4 dBi (outer ring), efficiencies of 84.6 % (inner) and 72.5 % (outer), and radiation patterns that exhibit perpendicular polarization and distinct angular patterns for different modes.

### Key quantitative results (as stated in the paper)

- **Isolation:** ≥ –22 dB between adjacent elements (measured/ simulated).  
- **Coupling reduction:** ≈ 6.5 dB.  
- **Bandwidth:** 2:1 VSWR = 75 MHz (center 2.45 GHz); after adding half‑wave slots, 50 MHz → 80 MHz (+60%).  
- **Envelope correlation:** < 0.4 across the band.  
- **Peak gains:** 4 dBi (inner ring), 3.4 dBi (outer ring).  
- **Efficiencies:** 84.6 % (inner), 72.5 % (outer).
