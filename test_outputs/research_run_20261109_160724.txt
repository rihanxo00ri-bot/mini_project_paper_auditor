## Direct answer

The paper uses a **four-port collocated MIMO antenna design** built from **concentric square-ring microstrip patch antennas**. Three methods work together:

1. **Polarization and angle diversity** through multiple transverse magnetic (TM) ring modes, to get compactness and low element correlation.
2. **Isolation by a Complementary Split Ring Resonator (CSRR) etched in the ground plane** at the strong-coupling corners, to reduce mutual coupling.
3. **Envelope correlation coefficient computed from far-field radiation patterns** (Eq. 1), to verify MIMO diversity performance.

The work is validated by CST Microwave Studio simulation and by measurement of a fabricated prototype.

## Supporting evidence

**1. Concentric square-ring patches with diversity (Page 1, Page 2, Page 3)**
- The abstract describes a four-port MIMO antenna "designed using concentric square ring patch antennas," with all ports resonating at 2.4 GHz ISM band (Page 1).
- The Introduction states that "polarization and angle diversity techniques are employed in square ring patch antennas to have 4 element MIMO" (Page 1).
- Section II says the design "uses concentric ring patches resonating in different transverse magnetic modes" and started from "a single ring resonating in the two fundamental orthogonal modes for P1 and P2" (Page 1).
- Feed positions were chosen "so as to excite higher order orthogonal modes with high isolation between them" (Page 2).
- Results identify P1/P2 with TM10 and TM01 (degenerate modes), and P3/P4 with TM21 and TM12, based on current-path analysis in Fig. 6 (Page 3).
- Polarization diversity is supported by the observation that P1 and P2 have perpendicular polarization planes, and angle diversity by different radiation-pattern shapes for fundamental and higher-order modes (Page 3).

**2. Bandwidth enhancement by reactive loading (Page 2)**
- Slots are etched on each ring arm ("half wave length slots") to improve bandwidth. The paper says the bandwidth is enhanced "by 60% from 50 MHz to 80 MHz" (Fig. 4). Slit loading also reduces coupling between orthogonal higher-order ring modes.

**3. CSRR isolation structure (Page 2, Page 3)**
- Two CSRRs are etched on the ground plane "on corners of the antenna in between the two concentric square rings with a tilt angle of 45°," at points where coupling currents are stronger (Page 2).
- The design formula is taken from reference [9] (Marqués et al.) (Page 2). The paper does not reproduce the formula itself.
- Mechanism as described by the authors: the CSRR responds to the z-directed magnetic field, disturbs ground currents, and reverses the ground-current polarity on the adjacent ring by nearly 180°. This makes the vertically polarized waves of P1 and P4 out of phase, giving least coupling (Page 2, Fig. 5).
- Ground current distributions at 2.45 GHz are compared with and without CSRR (Fig. 6, Page 3).
- Reported results: coupling between P1 and P4 is reduced by 6.5 dB (Page 3), and isolation is at least −22 dB (Page 4, Conclusion).

**4. Measurement and simulation setup (Page 1, Page 3)**
- Simulation: CST Microwave Studio (Page 1).
- Prototype: S-parameters measured with an R&S ZVB20 PNA vector network analyzer. Radiation patterns measured in an anechoic chamber by exciting one port while terminating the other three with matched loads (Page 3).
- Substrate: FR4, εr = 4.4, tanδ = 0.02, thickness 1.6 mm (Page 1).

**5. Envelope correlation from radiation patterns (Page 4)**
- The paper states that correlation "can be computed from S parameters or from far field radiation patterns." The S-parameter method is simpler but "does not provide exact results," so the authors use the radiation-pattern method, Eq. (1) (Page 4).
- Eq. (1) integrates the cross-polarization-weighted products of the θ and φ field components of port i and port j over the solid angle Ω, with probability densities Pθ and Pφ and XPR = PV/PH. It is normalized by the self-terms of each port.
- The incident environment is modeled as Gaussian in elevation and uniform in azimuth, citing Vaughan and Andersen [10] (Page 4).
- Result: correlation is "below 0.4" in the operating band (Page 4, Fig. 11).

## Interpretation (my reasoning, not stated as such in the paper)

- The method is a combination of an **antenna geometry strategy** (concentric rings in different modes, which avoids half-wavelength spacing) and an **isolation strategy** (a ground-plane CSRR). The two are separate contributions: the ring modes set the diversity, and the CSRR handles the residual coupling between identically polarized ports.
- Eq. (1) is the standard radiation-pattern envelope correlation form. The paper does not derive it, and the notation reads as a cross-polarization-weighted inner product of the two ports' patterns. Note that the printed equation contains a possible extraction ambiguity in its normalization, so the exact form should be checked against the original PDF.

## Limitations of the paper's description

- The CSRR design formula is delegated to reference [9]; the paper does not give the resonant-frequency equations or the full CSRR parameter derivation.
- The paper does not define the "reactive loading" mechanism quantitatively.
- The two bandwidth gains reported (about 40% for W5 = 6.6 mm on Page 2, and 60% from 50 to 80 MHz with slots on Page 2) are stated in different contexts; the paper does not clearly separate them.
- Fig. 1 to Fig. 11 are referenced, but I have only the extracted text, so I cannot verify the plotted values.
