**Answer – What method was used?**  
The authors developed and validated a **four‑port MIMO antenna built from concentric square‑ring patch elements, with “complementary split‑ring resonators” (CSRRs) etched on the ground plane to suppress mutual coupling**. Their overall workflow combines analytical/simulation design, physical realization of the CSRRs, and experimental verification.

### Summary of the method
1. **Concentric square‑ring patch design** (outer ring width W₄=27.4 mm, inner ring W₅=6.6 mm, substrate εᵣ=4.4, thickness 1.6 mm).  
2. **Load each ring with complementary split‑ring resonators (CSRRs)** etched on the ground plane at the two corner points between the rings, tilted ~45°.  
3. **Optionally add λ/2 slots** on the ring arms to raise bandwidth from ~50 MHz to ~80 MHz.  
4. **Simulate** all geometries (including CSRRs) in **CST Microwave Studio**.  
5. **Measure** the realized antenna’s S‑parameters with a VNA (R&S ZVB20 PNA) and obtain far‑field radiation patterns.  
6. **Quantify diversity** by calculating envelope correlation coefficients using the provided integral expression (Eq. 1) or from the measured patterns.  

Thus, the primary *method* employed to create the high‑isolation, compact four‑port MIMO system is the **loading of complementary split‑ring resonators on the ground plane at strategic corners of concentric square‑ring patches**, combined with **CST‑based simulation and experimental validation**.
