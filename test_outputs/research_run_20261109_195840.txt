**What the researcher discovered**

The authors presented a **compact four‑port MIMO antenna** built from **concentric square‑ring microstrip patches** that are excited in orthogonal transverse‑magnetic (TM) modes.  
To combat the high mutual coupling that occurs when identically polarized elements are placed very close together, they **etched a pair of sub‑wavelength Complementary Split Ring Resonators (CSRRs) on the ground plane at the corners of the rings**. The CSRRs:

* Reverse the polarity of the ground‑plane currents on adjacent rings (Fig. 5, p. 3–4).  
* Create a π‑phase shift between vertically polarized space waves of neighboring ports, thereby cancelling the coupled fields.  
* Reduce the coupling energy by **≈ 6.5 dB**, yielding an **isolation of at least –22 dB** between any two adjacent ports (p. 1, p. 3, p. 4).  

Consequences of this isolation:

* All four ports resonate at **2.45 GHz** with a **2:1 VSWR bandwidth of 75 MHz** (≈ 40 % wider than conventional square patches) (p. 1, p. 2).  
* Slit‑loading on the rings further improves bandwidth and suppresses coupling (Fig. 4, p. 2).  
* Measured efficiency is 84.6 % (inner ring) and 72.5 % (outer ring) in the 2.4 GHz ISM band (p. 4).  
* Far‑field patterns show **orthogonal polarization planes** for ports P₁/P₂ and **different radiation shapes** for higher‑order ports P₃/P₄, giving both **polarization diversity** and **angle diversity** (p. 4).  
* Envelope‑correlation coefficients between waveforms are **below 0.4** across the operating band, indicating strong MIMO diversity (Eq. (1); p. 5).  

In short, the paper’s main contribution is a **simple, non‑periodic sub‑wavelength four‑port MIMO system** that achieves:
* High isolation (≥ –22 dB) between co‑located elements,
* Low inter‑element correlation (< 0.4),
* Broadband operation (75 MHz @ 2.45 GHz),
* Compact size (60 × 60 mm × 1.6 mm³) and easy fabrication (no extra metallic parts beyond the CSRRs).
