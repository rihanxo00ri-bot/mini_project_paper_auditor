## Direct Answer

The method used in this paper is a **CSRR-loaded concentric square ring MIMO antenna design** for mutual-coupling/isolation enhancement in a four-port MIMO system.

Specifically, the paper's proposed method combines:

1. **A concentric square ring patch antenna architecture** (four ports generated from nested square rings resonating in multiple transverse magnetic modes), and
2. **A Complementary Split Ring Resonator (CSRR) etched on the ground plane** as the isolation/mutual-coupling reduction technique.

This is stated directly in the paper, not merely my interpretation.

## Evidence from the Paper

### 1. The core method — CSRR loading on the ground plane for isolation

- **Abstract (p.1):** The paper explicitly states the design approach: *"A novel four port multiple-input-multiple-output (MIMO) antenna system is designed using concentric square ring patch antennas... This design uses complementary split ring resonator (CSRR) loaded on its ground plane for enhanced isolation of -22 dB between patch antenna elements. CSRR reduces mutual coupling by about 6.5 dB."*

- **Section III — Proposed Isolation Structure (pp.2–3):** The method details are given here. The isolation strategy involves etching CSRRs on the ground plane: *"In order to reduce coupling currents CSRR’s are etched on the ground plane between the square rings using the design formula in [9]. The proposed structure consists of two CSRRs etched on corners of the antenna in between the two concentric square rings with a tilt angle of 450."*

- The mechanism, as reported by the authors: *"CSRR exhibits a resonant property in the presence of vertically polarized electric fields... This creates an electromotive force inside the antenna and disturbs the induced currents inside the ground plane. As a result the ground currents on the adjacent ring changes its polarity by nearly 1800... Thus vertically polarized space waves of P1 and P4 are out of phase now which ensures least coupling between them."*

- **Section IV — Results (p.3):** The effectiveness of the method is reported: *"Ground coupling currents are diminished significantly resulting in an isolation enhancement of 6.5 dB."* and *"The coupling between identically polarized elements P1 and P4 is reduced by 6.5 dB with the isolation structure."*

- **Section VI — Conclusion (p.4):** The authors summarize the method: *"A non-periodic subwavelength resonant structure CSRR is etched on ground at the ring corners to reduce mutual coupling between identically polarized elements."*

### 2. Antenna architecture method (supporting the overall method)

- **Section II — Antenna Design (p.2):** The method begins with a single square ring resonating in two fundamental orthogonal modes (TM10 and TM01), then a second concentric ring is added to create a four-port system. Feed positions are chosen *"so as to excite higher order orthogonal modes with high isolation between them"* (referring to TM21/TM12 modes for ports P3/P4).

## Final Answer (Complete Content)

The method used in this paper is the **proposed CSRR-loaded concentric square ring MIMO antenna isolation technique**. The authors designed a four-port MIMO antenna using **concentric square ring patch antennas** whose ports resonate at 2.4/2.45 GHz, and to reduce mutual coupling between closely spaced, identically polarized elements, they **etched a complementary split ring resonator (CSRR) on the ground plane** at the coupling-prone regions (the ring corners, with a 45° tilt angle). The CSRR acts as a resonant structure that disturbs the antenna ground currents, reverses their polarity by nearly 180°, and thereby reduces coupling. The reported outcome was an isolation enhancement of about **6.5 dB** and an achieved isolation of **-22 dB**, with all four ports resonating at **2.45 GHz** with a **2:1 VSWR bandwidth of 75 MHz**.

**Key page references:**
- Isolation method & CSRR loading: **pp.1, 2–3** (Abstract; Section III)
- Mutual coupling reduction / simulation results: **p.3** (Section IV)
- Conclusion summarizing the method: **p.4** (Section VI)

The paper therefore reports a **CSRR-loaded concentric square ring MIMO antenna design** as the method for achieving high isolation in a compact four-port MIMO system.
