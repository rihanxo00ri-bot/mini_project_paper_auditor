## What the Researchers Discovered

The researchers discovered that **etching Complementary Split Ring Resonators (CSRRs) on the ground plane at specific coupling hotspots between concentric square ring patch antennas effectively reduces mutual coupling in a four-port MIMO antenna system by approximately 6.5 dB**. This finding is supported by multiple lines of evidence from the paper:

### Key Experimental Findings:

1. **Isolation Enhancement**: The CSRR structure achieves isolation of **-22 dB between patch antenna elements**, representing a 6.5 dB improvement over designs without CSRR (Page 3, lines 2-4).

2. **Mechanism of Action**: The CSRR etched on the ground plane **reverses the polarity of coupling currents on the shared ground by nearly 180°**, which ensures minimum coupling between ports with the same polarization direction (Page 3, lines 9-15; Page 5, lines 12-18).

3. **Physical Evidence**: Surface current distribution analysis shows "coupling currents are diminished significantly" when CSRR is loaded on the ground plane (Page 3, lines 18-21).

4. **Frequency Stability**: The CSRR-loaded design maintains resonance at 2.45 GHz with minimal frequency shift (Page 3, line 22).

5. **MIMO Performance Validation**: Correlation coefficients computed from the antenna's far-field radiation patterns remain **below 0.4 across the operating band**, ensuring good MIMO diversity performance (Page 4, lines 16-21).

### Technical Achievement:

The researchers successfully designed and fabricated a **four-port MIMO system with overall dimensions of 60 x 60 x 1.6 mm³** operating in the 2.4 GHz ISM band, with all four ports resonating at 2.45 GHz and achieving the specified isolation improvements (Abstract; Page 1, lines 1-4; Page 2, lines 10-12).
