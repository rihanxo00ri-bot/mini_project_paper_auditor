**Answer – What the researcher discovered**

The authors of *Antennas and Wireless Propagation Letters* (IEEE, DOI 10.1109/LAWP.2015.2499322) presented a **novel four‑port MIMO antenna built from concentric square‑ring patch antennas that are “loaded” with complementary split‑ring resonators (CSRRs) on their ground plane**. Their experiments and simulations revealed several concrete findings:

| # | Discovery | Key quantitative result | Where it appears in the paper |
|---|-----------|------------------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | **Compact, collocated MIMO operation** – four identical ports can all resonate at the same frequency (2.45 GHz) inside a 60 × 60 × 1.6 mm³ footprint. | All four ports resonate at 2.4 GHz ISM band; VSWR bandwidth ≥ 2:1 (75 MHz, ≈ 40 % improvement over a single ring). | **Page 1** (abstract); **Page 2** (dimensions & resonance). |
| 2 | **High isolation achieved by CSRRs** – placing a pair of CSRRs at the ring corners reverses the polarity of the ground‑plane currents, turning vertically polarized space waves out of phase and creating a band‑stop at the operating frequency. | Isolation between adjacent identically‑polarized elements is **≥ –22 dB**; mutual coupling is reduced by **≈ 6.5 dB**. | **Page 1** (abstract); **Page 3** (current distribution & isolation enhancement). |
| 3 | **Bandwidth enhancement via slit loading** – adding half‑wave slots on each ring arm raises the operating bandwidth from 50 MHz to 80 MHz (≈ 60 % increase). | Bandwidth enhanced from 50 MHz to 80 MHz; S₁₁ shift to lower frequencies. | **Page 2** (“slit loading also reduces the coupling… enhances bandwidth … from 50 MHz to 80 MHz”). |
| 4 | **Low inter‑element correlation** – envelope correlation coefficients between waveforms stay below 0.4 across the band, guaranteeing good MIMO diversity. | Correlation coefficients **< 0.4** (see Fig. 11). | **Page 5** (definition of ρ_ij) and **Page 6** (result discussion). |

### Detailed evidence from the paper

- **Abstract (Page 1):**  
  > “This article has been accepted … Citation … DOI 10.1109/LAWP.2015.2499322, IEEE … Abstract—A novel four port MIMO antenna system is designed using concentric square ring patch antennas. All four ports are made to resonate at 2.4 GHz ISM band with an overall system dimension of 60 × 60 × 1.6 mm³. This design uses complementary split ring resonator (CSRR) loaded on its ground plane for enhanced isolation of **–22 dB** between patch antenna elements. **CSRR reduces mutual coupling by about 6.5 dB.** The antenna operates with a 2:1 VSWR bandwidth of **75 MHz** centered at 2.45 GHz.”  

- **Design & bandwidth (Page 2):**  
  > “A VSWR bandwidth of 75 MHz, about 40 % improvement … W5 increased to 6.6 mm … To improve bandwidth reactive loading … half wave slots … Thus the operating bandwidth is enhanced by 60 % from 50 MHz to 80 MHz as shown in Fig. 4. Slit loading also reduces the coupling between orthogonal higher order ring modes.”  

- **Isolation mechanism & current reversal (Page 3):**  
  > “There are mainly two sources of mutual coupling … CSRR’s are etched on the ground plane … The proposed structure consists of two CSRRs etched on corners … with a tilt angle of 450°. … When an electromagnetic wave is incident … the magnetic field along Z interacts with the CSRR … creates an electromotive force … changes its polarity by nearly 180° which can be observed from ground current distribution in Fig. 5(b). Thus vertically polarized space waves of P1 and P4 are out of phase now which ensures least coupling between them. The disturbance can change the characteristics such as equivalent inductance and capacitance thus obtaining the band stop at the frequency of interest.”  

- **Current distribution simulation (Page 3, Fig. 6):**  
  > “From Fig. 6, it can be observed that current path is equal to half wavelength for P1 and P2 … For P3 and P4 the current flow path has two maxima’s … This guarantees the excitation of TM21 and TM12 modes respectively … Current distribution with CSRR on ground provides clear evidence of improved isolation between the two ring patches. Ground coupling currents are diminished significantly resulting in an isolation enhancement of **6.5 dB**.”  

- **S‑parameter results (Pages 7–8, Fig. 7–8):**  
  > “Simulated S parameters of MIMO antenna without and with CSRR on ground is shown in Fig. 7 and 8. The coupling between identically polarized elements P1 and P4 is reduced by **6.5 dB** with the isolation structure. Same is the case with S23 … All the four ports resonate at 2.45 GHz with a minimum 2:1 VSWR bandwidth of 75 MHz. The fabricated prototype and measured S parameters in R&S ZVB20 PNA vector network analyzer, is shown in Fig. 9. Reasonable agreement between the simulated and measured results are achieved. Measured peak gain values of inner ring modes and outer ring modes are **4 dBi** and **3.4 dBi** respectively. Peak efficiency obtained for inner ring ports are **84.6 %**, and outer ring ports are about **72.5 %** in the 2.4 GHz ISM band.”  

- **Envelope correlation (Page 5):**  
  > “It is observed that within the operating band correlation coefficients are **below 0.4** which ensures good MIMO performance. 𝜌_ij = … (equation (1))”.  

- **Radiation patterns / angle diversity (Page 6, Fig. 10):**  
  > “Far field radiation pattern measurements … shows that feed ports P1 and P2 have polarization planes perpendicular to each other which ensures least coupling between ports. The same polarization diversity can also be observed between ring antenna ports P3 and P4. The shape of the pattern is different for fundamental and higher order modes, so that they radiate differently which guarantees **angle diversity**.”  

- **Prototype verification (Page 9):**  
  > “Fabricated prototype and measured S parameters … reasonable agreement between the simulated and measured results are achieved.”  

- **Conclusion (Page 6):**  
  > “A four port MIMO using concentric square rings resonating in multiple modes is presented here. A non-periodic subwavelength resonant structure CSRR is etched on ground at the ring corners to reduce mutual coupling between identically polarized elements. The dimensions of rings antenna and CSRR are adjusted so that all four ports resonate at the same frequency of 2.45 GHz with a high isolation of at least **–22 dB** between its individual elements. CSRR reverses the polarity of coupling current on common shared ground so as to improve isolation between elements having polarization along the same direction. The proposed antenna system has simple structure and the isolation technique employed does not include any additional metallic parts or require any intricate fabrication.”

### Summary of the core contribution

The researchers **discovered** that a **concentric arrangement of square‑ring microstrip patches, each excited in orthogonal higher‑order TM modes, combined with complementary split‑ring resonators etched on the ground corners, can simultaneously deliver**:

1. **Compact size** (~60 mm × 60 mm × 1.6 mm³),
2. **High isolation** (≥ –22 dB) and **mutual‑coupling reduction** (~6.5 dB),
3. **Wide, flat-bandwidth operation** (75 MHz VSWR bandwidth, up to 80 MHz after slot loading),
4. **Low inter‑element correlation** (envelope correlation < 0.4),
5. **Natural polarization and angle diversity** via orthogonal mode excitation,
6. **Simple, non‑periodic, sub‑wavelength fabrication** (only CSRRs on the ground).

These findings were validated both by full‑wave simulations (CST Microwave Studio) and by measurements on a physically fabricated prototype using a R&S ZVB20 PNA VNA (Figures 9, 10).
